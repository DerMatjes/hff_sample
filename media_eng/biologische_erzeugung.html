Biological production

The biological production of hydrogen using blue-green algae

To achieve complete decarbonization, large quantities of greenhouse gas-neutral energy sources are neened as soon as possible. Especially green hydrogen is an essential energy source that can be produced not only industrially but also with the help of blue-green algae (a type of cyanobacteria).

To produce green hydrogen, the algae utilize photobiological hydrogen production. Here there are two mechanisms involved, each one with a different enzyme. One is nitrogenase and the other is hydrogenase. In the former, nitrogenase serves to fix nitrogen, that is, to convert atmospheric Nitrogen (N₂) into biologically usable Ammonia. Here in this process, hydrogen is produced als a byproduct. To some extent, algae can reuse the produced hydrogen via uptake-hydrogenase and thus recover energy. However the amount of hydrogen released is rather small. Some cyanobacteria also possess the bidirectional hydrogenase, which is able to produce hydrogen directly. This process occurs when the cyanobacteria are suddenly exposed to too much light. Photosynthetic production is now proceeding very intensively, which is why many electrons are produced, but the cells cannot process them quickly enough. To prevent irreversible damage to the cells, the hydrogenase transfers excess electrons to protons from the surrounding environment. The hydrogenase thus serves as an electron acceptor here. The enzyme now reduces the protons to hydrogen. However, the enzyme (bidirectional hydrogenase) can also catalyze the reaction in the opposite direction. In this way, the enzyme can take up available hydrogen and oxidize it again, releasing electrons that can be used for photosynthesis and cellular respiration.



An issue with this process is the oxygen produced as a byproduct of photosynthesis. Though the enzyme hydrogenase is extremely vulnerable to oxygen, which is why even small amount can lead to irreversible damage, preventing the production of hydrogen. There are multiple approaches to remove said oxygen. One option is to remove the oxygen from the system after it has been produced. Although, this requires additional technical or chemical processes, making the process more complex.

On the other hand, there is an approach that involves removing the oxygen before it reaches the enzymes. This makes sustainable and self-sufficient hydrogen production using blue-green algae entirely feasible.
